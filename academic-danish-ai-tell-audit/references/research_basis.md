# Research basis — academic-danish-ai-tell-audit

Confidence-tagged. **[verified]** checked against a primary source; **[adaptation]**
informed extrapolation, not yet validated for Danish.

## Evidence that AI tells are real and measurable (English)
- **Kobak, González-Márquez, Horvát & Lause (2025), Science Advances (arXiv 2406.07016).
  [verified]** ~14–15M PubMed abstracts; abrupt post-ChatGPT frequency surge in style
  words (delve, underscore, showcasing, potential, crucial, findings); ≥13.5% of 2024
  abstracts LLM-processed. Method: *excess vocabulary* — signal is excess frequency, not
  presence.
- **Liang et al. (2024), ICML (arXiv 2403.07183). [verified]** 6.5–16.9% of ML peer-review
  sentences substantially LLM-modified; characteristic adjectives (commendable,
  meticulous, intricate, innovative, comprehensive).

## The Danish gap (honest limitation)
- **No published Danish excess-vocabulary corpus exists to our knowledge (as of build).
  [adaptation]** The Danish marker list is extrapolated from (a) the English findings via
  common calques and (b) reported Danish LLM connectives/phrasings. It MUST be calibrated
  against a Danish baseline before its density numbers are treated as authoritative.
  → The list is versioned; recommendations are advisory; the metric flags, never rewrites.

## Design consequences
1. Measure excess/over-use, not presence (Kobak). 2. No document-level authorship claim.
3. Versioned lexicon (moving target). 4. Register-aware; rewriting deferred to
   academic-danish-klarsprog. 5. Integrity: not for evading disclosure; fidelity preserved.
