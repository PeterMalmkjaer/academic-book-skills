# Research basis — academic-danish-engagement-audit

Confidence-tagged. **[verified]** checked during build; **[classic]** established;
**[adaptation]** informed extrapolation to Danish, not yet validated.

## Validated core (English) + Danish adaptation
- **Flesch, R. (1948/1949). Human-Interest formula.** HI = 3.635·(personal words/100) +
  0.314·(personal sentences/100). [verified] Coefficients are English-calibrated; applying
  them to Danish yields an **adapted, relative** index (not a validated Danish instrument).
  [adaptation] Personal words = pronouns, gendered/person nouns; personal sentences =
  quotes, questions, direct address, exclamations.

## Text features that drive reading interest (language-general theory; Danish proxies)
- **Concreteness / imageability = strongest text-based interest predictor** (Sadoski,
  Goetz & Rodriguez 2000, JEP 92; dual-coding theory). [verified]
- **Situational interest** via coherence, vividness, character identification, suspense,
  novelty (Schraw & Lehman 2001, EPR 13; Hidi & Baird 1988). [verified]
- **Reading motivation / interest development** (Hidi & Renninger 2006; Guthrie et al.
  2006, RRQ). [verified] · **Narrative aids engagement** (Dahlstrom 2014, PNAS). [classic]

## Mandatory caveat: seductive details
- **Harp & Mayer (1998), JEP 90(3), 414–434; meta-analysis Sundararajan & Adesope (2020),
  EPR 32.** [verified] Interesting-but-irrelevant detail *reduces* recall/transfer. → the
  score rewards RELEVANT concreteness and coherence, never trivia.

## Danish gap (honest limitation)
No validated Danish Human-Interest calibration or Danish text-interest corpus is used here.
The Danish marker lists are an informed adaptation; **calibrate against Danish material**
before treating numbers as absolute. Versioned; recommendations advisory; measure only.
