# Rubrics: 19 criteria in 5 dimensions

All criteria are scored 0–4. Levels 0, 2 and 4 are described; 1 and 3 are intermediate.
Anchors are phrased as OBSERVABLE text properties (the BARS principle) — score what you can
point to, not an overall impression. Evidence base per criterion: see `references.md`.

## Dimension S — Semantic content

**S1 Definitional practice** *(Podsakoff et al. 2016; Suddaby 2010)*
- 0: Central concepts are used without definition, or definitions are circular ("governance is what governs").
- 2: Most key concepts are defined, but some only after pages of use, or only via examples.
- 4: Every key concept is defined at first occurrence with attributes AND delimitation (what falls outside).

**S2 Conceptual consistency** *(Suddaby 2010)*
- 0: The same term is used in clearly different senses without marking; synonyms alternate freely for the same concept.
- 2: Core terms are held consistent; peripheral terms drift (e.g. "goal"/"target"/"objective" interchangeably).
- 4: One term per concept throughout; deliberate shifts of meaning are explicitly marked.

**S3 Concept relations** *(Suddaby 2010)*
- 0: Key concepts sit side by side with no statement of how they relate.
- 2: The main relations are stated, but their type (causal? constitutive? hierarchical?) stays unclear.
- 4: Relations between key concepts are made explicit with their type, and used consistently in the argument.

**S4 Argumentative precision** *(Hyland & Tse 2004; partly this skill's operationalisation)*
- 0: Strong claims ("always", "proves") without covering evidence; or pervasive hedging that empties the claims.
- 2: Claim strength and evidence mostly match; a few uncovered quantifiers or empty hedges.
- 4: Each claim's modality is calibrated to its evidence; hedging is informative ("provided that X").

## Dimension H — Hermeneutic content

Special rule: H1 and H2 require at least TWO quotes per score, and confidence can never exceed medium.

**H1 Interpretive depth** *(Kintsch 1988; Tracy 2010)*
- 0: Pure paraphrase — the text renders theories without connecting, contrasting or reinterpreting them.
- 2: Some independent connections or contrasts, but the bulk is summary.
- 4: The text builds an independent interpretive model: theories are reinterpreted, tensions between them addressed.

**H2 Quality of synthesis** *(Tracy 2010, meaningful coherence)*
- 0: Parts are summarised sequentially; no unifying point beyond "we have now seen …".
- 2: A unifying point exists but adds little beyond the sum of the parts.
- 4: The synthesis contains a point no single part holds, and the parts are visibly necessary to it.

**H3 Reflexivity** *(Tracy 2010, sincerity; Lincoln & Guba 1985)*
- 0: Interpretive choices are presented as self-evident; no marking of position or alternatives.
- 2: Position is marked in places; obvious alternative readings are not mentioned.
- 4: Interpretive choices and position are made explicit, and obvious alternative readings acknowledged and handled.

**H4 Evidential grounding** *(Nowell et al. 2017; Lincoln & Guba 1985)*
- 0: Interpretations with no trace to the interpreted material ("the book essentially shows that …" without reference).
- 2: Most interpretations can be traced; a few hang free.
- 4: Every interpretation can be traced to concrete places (chapter/section/quote) in the interpreted material.

## Dimension T — Technical composition

**T1 Global structure** *(Kintsch 1988; Graesser et al. 2004)*
- 0: Section order appears arbitrary; reshuffling would not harm the argument.
- 2: The main parts follow the argument's logic; some sections have unclear function in the whole.
- 4: Every section has an identifiable function, and the order visibly mirrors the argument's logic.

**T2 Local cohesion** *(Graesser et al. 2004; Halliday & Hasan 1976)*
- 0: Abrupt transitions; new actors/concepts introduced as if known; threads dropped.
- 2: Transitions mostly present; referential continuity breaks in places.
- 4: Explicit transitions; concepts and actors are resumed recognisably sentence-to-sentence and section-to-section.

**T3 Metadiscourse/signposting** *(Hyland & Tse 2004)*
- 0: No reader guidance, OR guidance that promises what the text does not deliver.
- 2: Guidance at major transitions; redundant in places ("as mentioned … as previously mentioned").
- 4: Forward- and backward-pointing markers exactly where needed, covering the text's actual content, without redundancy.

**T4 Textual economy** *(Sundararajan & Adesope 2020)*
- 0: Several passages with no function for the argument (anecdotes, digressions) without marking.
- 2: A few functionless passages, or excursuses not marked as such.
- 4: No passage can be removed without loss to the argument; excursuses are explicitly marked.

## Dimension M — Metaphor use

**M1 Functionality** *(Lakoff & Johnson 1980; Taylor & Dewsbury 2018)*
- 0: Metaphors are ornament; they explain nothing abstract any better.
- 2: The bearing metaphors work; a fair number of decorative metaphors without explanatory power.
- 4: Every bearing metaphor explains something abstract via something known, and the text actively exploits the mapping.

**M2 Mapping precision** *(Lakoff & Johnson 1980)*
- 0: The source domain does not fit the target; the metaphor ascribes properties the target lacks.
- 2: The mapping holds at the core; peripheral implications (which the text does not disown) limp.
- 4: The mapping holds, and the text itself marks where the metaphor stops applying.

**M3 Consistency** *(operationalised from M2; general style criterion — low evidential weight)*
- 0: Mixed metaphors within a passage ("navigate the foundation"); bearing metaphors shift without motivation.
- 2: Bearing metaphors are kept apart; a few unmotivated shifts.
- 4: Bearing metaphors are sustained throughout; shifts are motivated and marked.

**M4 Framing awareness** *(Thibodeau & Boroditsky 2013)*
- 0: Value-laden metaphors (war, disease, machine, market) used on contested topics without marking.
- 2: The framing is reasonable for the genre but unreflected.
- 4: On value-laden topics the text shows awareness of the metaphor's framing (chooses, marks or counterbalances it).

## Dimension F — Tables and figures

Automatically inactive if the text has no tables/figures (use the "prose only" weights).

**F1 Self-containment and apparatus** *(Franzblau & Chung 2012)*
- 0: Missing number/title/source; abbreviations unexplained; body text does not refer to it.
- 2: Apparatus in place, but the title is not informative, or some references are missing/wrong.
- 4: Number, informative title, source, explained abbreviations; readable alone; body text refers explicitly and correctly.

**F2 Format–message match** *(Shah & Hoeffner 2002; Ratwani et al. 2008)*
- 0: The format works against the point (trend in a table; 3D pie for comparison; a figure where the numbers were the point).
- 2: The format is acceptable but not optimal for the message type.
- 4: Format matches message (trend→line, comparison→bar, distribution→histogram, structure→diagram), and the table-vs-figure choice is content-driven.

**F3 Graphical economy** *(Tufte 1983, normative; Sundararajan & Adesope 2020)*
- 0: Decorative elements without data function dominate; truncated axes unmarked; misleading scales.
- 2: Mostly clean; a few needless elements or unmarked axis choices.
- 4: All ink carries data; axes, scales and labels truthful and marked.

**F4 Text–figure integration** *(Mayer 2019; Franzblau & Chung 2012)*
- 0: The figure is not mentioned, or contradicts the body text.
- 2: The figure is mentioned but not interpreted — the reader must extract the point alone.
- 4: The body text refers to, interprets and USES the figure in the argument; figure and text tell the same story.
