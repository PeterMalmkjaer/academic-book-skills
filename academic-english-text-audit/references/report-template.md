# Report template

Use EXACTLY this structure. The fixed format is what makes runs comparable across chapters
and over time. Language: English, academic register, no complimentary filler.

# Text audit: [title/chapter]

## How to read this report

> This audit was produced by a language model (LLM) using an anchored assessment rubric.
> A language model has limitations: it can overlook, misweigh and misread. The report is
> therefore a **dialogue tool** — structured input for the author's and editor's further
> work — and must **never stand alone** as a final verdict on the text.
>
> **About the numbers:** The scale does not follow the school scale. 50 % corresponds to
> the standard of the chosen benchmark (here: [baseline]); 75–89 % is well above the
> standard and the realistic target zone; 90 % and above is exemplar level, rare by design
> and not the working target; 100 % is a theoretical maximum. A high number does not by
> itself make the text ready for publication — that is decided by whether the list of
> binding constraints at the end is empty.

## Method box
- This audit is a structured second opinion from a language-model judge with an anchored
  rubric — not a psychometrically validated measurement instrument. It is a dialogue tool
  for further human processing and never stands alone; decisions about the text belong to
  the author and editor.
- Scores hold ONLY against the chosen calibration ([profile] + baseline [x]); they cannot
  be compared with runs under any other calibration.
- Confidence bands are conventions (±3/±6/±10 pp by criterion confidence), not statistical
  intervals.
- Criteria scored as "estimate" (without evidence): [list or "none"].
- H1/H2 are by design at most medium confidence (interpretive depth is disciplined judgment).
- Evidence base for the criteria: see the skill's reference list (Jonsson & Svingby 2007;
  Suddaby 2010; Tracy 2010; etc.).
